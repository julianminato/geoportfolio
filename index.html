About

This website is the geoportfolio of Julian Minato. The website contains information that is academically and professionally related to the field of geospatial science

I am an aspiring geospatial scientist currently in my final year of studying a Bachelor of Geospatial Science (Honours) degree at RMIT University. My geospatial-related interests are anything Cartography related - I love the map making process and being able to visualise information in a manner that anyone can interpret. I am also interested in the Transport Industry (particularly rail) and love working with spatial data surrounding Transport Infrastructure. I am currently working on my Capstone geospatial project of which my I am analysing & mapping spatial accessibility to public open spaces in Greater Melbourne. I am also currently working in the urban planning sector as a Geospatial Intern with @Leisure planners in which I am helping develop, analyse and maintain various types of spatial data to help produce industry ready cartographic outputs for clients.

Academic Portfolio Overview - Work and Maps from my Studies

Cloud-based Open Source GIS

This project showcases a web-based GIS I built to map and route to public drinking fountains across the City of Melbourne using open data and open-source tools. I developed the full system architecture, developing a PostGIS database, a Python Flask backend, and a Leaflet web app that connects to routing and geocoding APIs from OpenStreetMap. The app lets users find their nearest fountain and generate walking routes directly on the map. It highlights my ability to manage spatial databases, integrate APIs, and build functional web-mapping tools to solve real, location-based problems. Capstone Project

This project examined spatial equity in access to public open space (POS) across Greater Melbourne using a GIS-based suitability analysis. By integrating walkable network service areas, population weighting, and a custom POS quality classification, the model assessed how equitably high-quality parks are distributed relative to population demand. I implemented the analysis using OpenStreetMap data, Victorian government datasets, and ground-truthed field observations, refining raw green space data into a quality-weighted geodatabase. The final outputs included accessibility heatmaps and gap analyses that highlight suburbs most in need of improved open space provision. Below are some outputs from the project:

  • Walking Distance to Nearest Open Space - Built using Service Area and Network Dataset in ArcGIS Pro
  • Inset of Previous Map showing the City of Melbourne

Cartography

Cartography consists of creating maps using spatial data obtained from sources such as publicly online government data and population numbers from the Australian Bureau of Statistics (ABS).
